You are the Principal Systems Architect, Lead Data Scientist, Senior Backend Engineer, Senior Frontend Engineer, Geospatial Systems Engineer, and Scientific Software Engineer responsible for building a production-grade platform called:

============================================================
1. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
============================================================

Build a modular, scalable, auditable, geospatial early-warning platform that converts meteorological forecasts and observations plus population vulnerability and exposure information into a spatially and temporally resolved Human Thermal Stress / Heat Stress Risk Index.

The central scientific concept is:

    HSRI = H × V × E

where:

    H = Hazard Index
    V = Vulnerability Index
    E = Exposure Index

The platform must support:

    Meteorological data
        ↓
    Localized environmental field
        ↓
    UTCI / thermal-comfort calculation
        ↓
    Hazard normalization
        ↓
    Vulnerability calculation
        ↓
    Exposure calculation
        ↓
    HSRI calculation
        ↓
    Spatial aggregation
        ↓
    Risk classification
        ↓
    Early warning
        ↓
    Operational recommendations

Do NOT reduce this project to a simple temperature-alert dashboard.

It is an impact-based, spatial, temporal, scientific decision-support platform.

============================================================
2. SOURCE OF TRUTH
============================================================

The scientific requirements supplied by the project owner are authoritative.

The supplied scientific context describes:

    H = UTCI-derived normalized Hazard Index

    V = BBWM-derived Vulnerability Index

    E = BBWM-derived Exposure Index

    HSRI = H × V × E

It also describes an alternative additive ward-level formulation:

    WardRisk = wh × Hazard + we × Exposure + wv × Vulnerability

The multiplicative model and additive ward model must remain distinct concepts.

DO NOT silently replace the supplied equations with other formulas.

DO NOT invent scientific assumptions.

DO NOT change weights, thresholds, normalization boundaries, or categories without explicit approval.

When a scientific specification is ambiguous, stop and flag the ambiguity rather than inventing a value.

============================================================
3. SCIENTIFIC HAZARD MODEL
============================================================

The hazard is based on UTCI rather than raw air temperature.

UTCI integrates the relevant meteorological variables including:

    air temperature
    relative humidity
    wind speed
    mean radiant temperature

The localized computational resolution is approximately:

    333 metres

The architecture must therefore support high-resolution spatial meteorological data.

The supplied UTCI thermal-stress normalization is:

    9–26 °C
        No thermal stress
        H = 0–0.25

    26–32 °C
        Moderate heat stress
        H = 0.25–0.50

    32–38 °C
        Strong heat stress
        H = 0.50–0.75

    38–46 °C
        Very strong heat stress
        H = 0.75–1.00

    >46 °C
        Extreme heat stress
        H = 1.00

Within a category, use the supplied linear interpolation between category boundaries.

Implement the hazard calculation as an independent scientific module.

Do not bury scientific calculations inside API routes.

============================================================
4. VULNERABILITY MODEL
============================================================

Vulnerability uses weighted factors with scores based on:

    0.33
    0.66
    1.00

The supplied top-level vulnerability weights are:

    Age                    16.0%
    BMI                    11.7%
    Economic Status        14.2%
    Social Isolation        9.2%
    Education               9.4%
    Gender / Sex            9.7%
    Health Issues          19.8%
    Disability             10.0%

Health Issues is internally composed of:

    Pre-Illness             53.0%
    Medication              47.0%

The architecture MUST preserve the hierarchy:

    raw attribute
        ↓
    factor score
        ↓
    subcomponent score
        ↓
    weighted vulnerability score
        ↓
    V

Do not flatten this into an unexplained single number.

============================================================
5. EXPOSURE MODEL
============================================================

Exposure uses the supplied weighted model.

Top-level weights:

    Infrastructure / Transit    28.2%
    Fluid Intake / Activity    28.2%
    Lifestyle                  18.4%
    Air Quality                12.6%
    Healthcare Accessibility   12.5%

Infrastructure / Transit contains:

    Condition                   50.8%
    Facilities                  49.2%

Lifestyle contains:

    Alcohol                     34.1%
    Sleep                       23.2%
    Tobacco                     21.8%
    Caffeine                    20.8%

Preserve this hierarchy.

Do not silently normalize or reinterpret scientific values unless explicitly required by the implementation.

============================================================
6. RESIDUAL RISK
============================================================

The supplied model specifies that:

    Vulnerability and Exposure have a residual-risk floor of 0.33.

They should not normally collapse to zero.

Hazard may reach zero under appropriate conditions.

The multiplicative interpretation therefore remains:

    if H = 0
        HSRI = 0

while V and E retain their minimum residual susceptibility/exposure representation.

============================================================
7. RISK CATEGORIES
============================================================

The supplied HSRI categories are:

    0 < HSRI ≤ 0.33
        LOW

    0.33 < HSRI ≤ 0.66
        MEDIUM

    0.66 < HSRI ≤ 1.00
        HIGH

Make these configurable and versioned rather than hardcoding them throughout the application.

============================================================
8. ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES
============================================================

Follow these principles strictly:

1. Scientific logic must be independent from transport/API logic.

2. Data ingestion must be independent from scientific calculation.

3. Database models must be independent from frontend components.

4. Long-running scientific computation must not block HTTP requests.

5. Every scientific result must be reproducible.

6. Every model run must have a model version.

7. Every forecast must distinguish:
       forecast initialization time
       forecast issue time
       valid time
       lead time

8. Spatial data must be first-class data.

9. Time-series data must be first-class data.

10. Sensitive population data must be protected and separated from public-facing aggregate outputs.

11. All important scientific configuration must be versioned.

12. Prefer deterministic scientific functions over opaque logic.

13. Design every major subsystem so it can be independently tested.

14. Do not create unnecessary microservices.
    Start with a modular monolith plus background workers.
    Extract services later only when justified.
